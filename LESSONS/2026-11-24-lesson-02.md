## New Vocabulary and Expressions

## Grammar Points Covered
- Present simple for routines vs past simple for completed events.
- Third person singular -s (she sleeps, this makes).
- Articles before countable nouns (a dinner → dinner).
- Prepositions in/on for time expressions.
- "Used to" for past ownership/habits.
- "Both" only for exactly two items.
- "I suppose" vs "supposing".
- "Heard of" vs "heard about".

## Recurring Difficulties
- Dropping small words (to, a, the) — the single most frequent issue across both lessons.
- Tense consistency when switching between routine and story.
- Preposition choice for calendar time.

## Strengths
- Strong willingness to self-correct and repeat.
- Can handle business scenarios (late project explanation) with coaching.
- Broad personal vocabulary (games, films, pets).
- Asks sharp questions about methodology and pronunciation.

## Concrete Takeaways
1. "To be honest" + past tense + "deliver on time" = a solid professional sentence.
2. "Fall behind" is the key phrase for delays; pair it with a reason clause.
3. Small words matter more than big vocabulary for sounding natural.
4. Dates: "on" for days, "in" for months and years.
5. "Chest" not "breast" in everyday contexts.
